# The facilitator's script

For the two sessions with **Taberna Santo Domingo** and **Conchi**, both in Ronda. Written in
English; every line meant to be spoken is quoted in Spanish, and is said as written.

The point of the session is to find out where someone hesitates. A session where the owner
understood everything and liked it all teaches nothing and probably means the questions were
leading.

## Before the session

**Confirm they have no website.** Both listings on Google show none, and a listing can simply be
incomplete. Ask on the phone or in person:

> «¿Tenéis página web, aunque sea antigua o de las que hace la propia Google?»

It changes the session either way, so it is asked first:
- **Never had one:** the questions below work as written.
- **Had one, or has one:** they are not a first-timer. Ask instead what the last one cost them,
  what happened to it, and who updated it. Someone who suffered the process once judges this
  product against that memory, not against nothing, and that has to be recorded with their
  answers rather than mixed in with the others.

**Ask for consent, plainly:**

> «Quiero enseñarte una herramienta que estamos haciendo y ver cómo la usarías. No es un examen:
> si algo no se entiende, el fallo es nuestro y justo eso es lo que busco. ¿Te importa que tome
> notas? No grabo nada y no te pido ningún dato.»

## The rules for the facilitator

- **Explain nothing.** Not what the screen is for, not where to click. If they ask "¿qué hago
  aquí?", answer with «¿Qué harías tú?» and write down that the question was needed.
- **Say nothing approving.** No "muy bien", no "exacto". They will start performing for you.
- **Do not defend the product.** If they say it looks like a template, write it down and ask them
  to say more.
- **Time it.** The dossier's promise is under ten minutes. Note where the clock actually goes.
- **Write down the words they use** for their own trade and services. That vocabulary is what the
  text bank and the sector list should be using.

## Screen by screen, what is watched

| Screen | What is watched |
|---|---|
| 1 — the name | Do they look for their logo? Do they understand the logo is optional? |
| 2 — the sector | Do they find their trade in the ten, or do they look for their exact case? Do they use the search field, or read the grid? Does anyone reach for "Otro sector"? |
| 3 — what you offer | **Do they believe the suggestions are already ticked?** This is the one question that has already been decided on paper: they start unticked, on purpose. Do they tick, or do they scroll past thinking it is done? Do they add one of their own? |
| 4 — where you are | Do they hesitate over the address? Does "no tengo local" make sense to the ones who have one? |
| 5 — the main action | Which action do they pick, and do they say why? Note it, because the site they are about to see does not use their answer (see the disclosures). |
| 6 — generating | Do they wait, or do they think it has frozen? |
| 7 — the three variants | **The decisive screen.** Do they choose, or freeze? How long? Do they look at all three, or take the highlighted one? Do they open "Ver a tamaño real"? Do they read the texts or only look at the shapes? |

## What is said out loud at screen 07

Four things are ours, not the product's, and staying quiet about them would buy a compliment we
did not earn. They are said once, in this order, before the owner is asked anything and **before
they touch the screen**:

> «Cuatro cosas antes de que mires. Las fotos son de muestra: las cambias tú después. Los
> servicios que aparecen los hemos marcado nosotros, porque tú no has rellenado el cuestionario
> de verdad. El botón grande dice "Visítanos" porque no hemos guardado tu teléfono: en la web
> real sería el que tú elijas en la última pregunta. Y ese botón todavía no lleva a ningún sitio:
> si lo pulsas no pasa nada, y no es que se haya roto.»

**The fourth one is said before, not after.** The main button is the most visible thing on the
page, so it will be pressed. A dead button discovered by pressing it turns the session into a
conversation about a technical fault, and the answer to "¿publicarías esto?" is then about the
fault instead of about the site. Said beforehand, it costs one sentence.

Nothing is said about the colours. If they bring the colour up by themselves, that is worth
writing down; asking about it would only measure our taste, since we chose the palette knowing
whose business it is, which the generator will not.

## One question at screen 07, before the closing ones

The catalog is about to get its "Contacto y reservas" section, and the biggest open decision in
it is whether a client's site needs a form at all. A published site is static: it cannot send a
form anywhere without depending on something, and ADR 0001 forbids that something being ours. So
the decision risks being taken by technical limitation. Two owners in front of us settle it
better. Asked while they are looking at their own site:

> «Cuando alguien quiere pediros cita o preguntar algo, ¿cómo te gusta que lo haga? ¿Te vale con
> que te llamen o te escriban por WhatsApp, o esperas que te escriban desde la web y que te
> llegue a ti?»

It is not said that a form is hard, and no option is offered as the reasonable one. What is
written down is which they name first, and whether the words "formulario" or "correo" come up
without being prompted.

## The questions at the end

In this order, and with silence after each one:

1. > «¿Publicarías esto tal y como está?»

   The answer that matters. "Sí, pero…" is a no with a reason: write the reason down verbatim.
2. > «¿Qué le falta?»
3. > «¿Qué le sobra?»
4. > «¿Estos textos suenan a los tuyos?»

   If they say it sounds generic, that is a finding about the text bank (ADR 0009), not a failure
   of the session.
5. > «Si esto costase un pago único, ¿por cuánto te parecería bien?»

   Asked last and never earlier, because a price mentioned early changes every answer before it.
   The number is not a decision — the price is still open — but where they place it is data.

## Which variants each one saw

The order rotates on purpose, so that a preference for the middle card cannot be mistaken for a
preference for a design:

| Business | Left | Middle, highlighted | Right |
|---|---|---|---|
| Taberna Santo Domingo | 1 Clásica | **2 Con foto grande** | 3 Compacta |
| Conchi | 2 Con foto grande | **3 Compacta** | 1 Clásica |

Record which one they chose **by name**, not by position.

## After the session

Write up the same day, before the memory tidies itself:
- the times: to finish the questionnaire, and to decide at screen 07;
- every hesitation, with the screen it happened on;
- their exact words for their trade and their services;
- what they said to the five closing questions, quoted;
- how they expect people to get in touch, and whether a form came up by itself;
- anything they tried to do that the screens did not allow.

Findings that touch a decision already recorded — the sector list of ADR 0010, the text bank of
ADR 0009, the photo criteria of ADR 0011, the cardinality of ADR 0013 — go to those ADRs as
amendments, not into a separate document nobody reads again.
