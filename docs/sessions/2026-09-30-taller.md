## What this session does not settle

Three limits, stated before the findings so nothing below is read as more than it is.

1. **There are no verbatim quotes.** The substance of every answer below is the facilitator's
   report of what the owner said; **the owner's own words are not on record for any of the five
   closing questions**, nor for the formatting answer. The first two sessions quote all five. This
   is the first limit rather than a footnote because of what the script puts above every other
   rule — «**the paraphrase in the first session cost a real answer**» — and because ADR 0024 was
   built to compare this answer against Conchi's, which *is* a quote. That comparison is now
   between a quotation and a summary. It is not nothing and it is not the same thing.
2. **The observation table is almost entirely blank, for the third time.** The script calls it
   «the point of this session» and Conchi's write-up explains why: the editor is where most of the
   clock goes and it has no direct observation behind it. Three sessions have now been run and the
   table is still empty. That is recorded here as a finding about the sessions, not about the
   product, and it is the one thing a fourth session should fix before it adds anything else.
3. **It is one business.** ADR 0017's framing holds: these are experiments of one subject each.
   Three is not a sample either. Nothing here is a rate or a tendency.

## The phase 1 acceptance criterion

The protocol states it as:

> «Una persona de fuera del equipo, sin explicación previa y sin ayuda, monta la web de un negocio
> real en menos de diez minutos. Se cronometra y se observa, no se pregunta.»

Clause by clause, and **two of them are not evidenced by this session**:

- **From outside the team** — a real business owner, per the facilitator's record.
- **A real business** — his own trade.
- **Under ten minutes** — six, to the downloaded ZIP. Second timed session, second time under.
- **Timed** — yes.
- **No prior explanation, no help** — **not recorded.** Conchi's session has the sentence that
  evidences this clause («El cliente no hizo preguntas y usó correctamente la app»); this one has
  no equivalent. The script instructs the facilitator to prepare nothing, which is an instruction
  and not a record that it was followed.
- **Observed** — only partly. It was timed; the observation the script asked for is not here.

**Declaring phase 1 accepted is direction's, not development's.** This session adds a second
timing under ten and takes nothing away. It does not add the unaided-use evidence, and the
write-up says so rather than letting six minutes stand in for it.

## The design-tools switch → ADR 0025

**This is the sharpest finding of the session, and it cuts both ways at once.**

What is recorded: **he saw the switch, he turned it on, and he used the floating toolbar.**

What the code makes of that is not a matter of interpretation, so it is set down exactly:

- The switch is a `role="switch"` button **34×19 pixels** with a **9-pixel** label,
  «Herramientas de diseño» (`apps/editor/src/editor/EditorShell.tsx`).
- **The question is never shown unprompted.** `onClick={() => (on ? onChange(false) : onAsk())}`:
  the panel appears only because somebody pressed the switch. Nothing offers it, nothing
  onboards it.
- What it then asks is **«¿Montas webs para otros?»**, with «Si es tu trabajo, te encendemos las
  herramientas de diseño: rejilla, colocación y ajustes por dispositivo.»
- He pressed **«Sí, enciéndelas»**.
- The switch is not offered below `MIN_STUDIO_WIDTH = 1024`. Since it was offered and pressed, the
  editor's window was **at least 1024px wide**. That is deduced from the gate in
  `designTools.ts`, not observed — it is the one thing in this write-up derived rather than
  recorded, and it is flagged as such because no session has ever recorded the device.

**The discoverability half of ADR 0025 holds, and by a wider margin than it deserved.** The ADR
§5 chose an absent control over a disabled one, and the switch it settled on is small, unlabelled
except in 9-pixel type, and self-service. The first owner ever to meet it found it and pressed it.

**The sorting half does not hold.** The question exists to tell a professional from an owner, and
it admitted the owner of a car workshop. He does not build websites for other people; the
question's own terms say the tools are «si es tu trabajo». **Why he said yes is not recorded**,
and the two readings the record cannot separate are that he did not read it, and that he read it
and wanted the tools regardless. Either way a question that offers more capability to whoever
answers yes will be answered yes, and that is a property of the gate rather than of this owner.

### And two of the seven things he asked for were already behind it

Of the seven he named, **`color exacto` and `más libertad en la posición` both exist**, and both
live behind the switch he had turned on. They do not live in the same place, and the difference is
the whole finding:

| What he asked for | Where it is | Was he in that place? |
|---|---|---|
| **`color exacto`** | the **floating toolbar**, `measures.exact` (`EXACT_TODAY` = `color`, `padding`, `borderRadius`) | **Yes — he used the toolbar** |
| **`más libertad en la posición`** | the **`Diseño` panel's** grid, from sprint 8 | **Not recorded** — whether he opened the panel is not in the record |

So for colour the control was inside a bar he is recorded as using, and he still named it. For
position the door was visible to him — the `Diseño` rail item appears once the tools are on — and
nothing says whether he went through it.

**What this does not establish is the order of events.** The formatting question is asked at the
end, after the closing questions; the switch could have been pressed at any point, and the record
has no timeline. So it cannot be said that the exact-colour control was in front of him at the
moment he named colour as missing. What it does establish is that by the end of the session he had
opted in, used the bar, and still had both on his list.

**Finding the switch is not the same as finding what is behind it**, and that is the first real
evidence ADR 0025 has about its own premise. It is not fixed by writing more controls.

### What this does and does not do to ADR 0024's waking conditions

ADR 0024 named two, and **neither is met at the letter**. Writing that they were would be the
script's own lesson committed in the other direction — turning an answer into a spontaneous
request.

- **«A second owner asks for it, in a session, without being prompted.»** **Not met as written.**
  Conchi's request was spontaneous: nobody asked her about formatting, she named it answering
  «¿Publicarías esto tal y como está?». This owner's came out of a question that points straight at
  the appearance of text, and the ADR's own script is what told the facilitator to ask it. It is a
  second owner asking; it is not a second owner asking unprompted.
- **«It survives the texts being fixed.»** **This one is met, and it is the stronger of the two.**
  The bank was signed before the session, he says the texts sound like his (below), and formatting
  is still what he names. The doubt #52 raised — «whether formatting is what she wants or what she
  reached for» — is answered: it was not vocabulary.

**So the case for building it rests on three things together, and on none of them alone:**

1. **Two owners have asked** — one spontaneously, one answering a question the ADR's own script
   required.
2. **The texts are already fixed** and the request survived them, which is the second waking
   condition met at the letter.
3. **The professional audience.** ADR 0025 made the professional editor a goal of the product. A
   bold run inside a sentence does not depend on an owner asking for it; whoever builds sites for
   other people needs it.

That is the shape of the evidence, and it is what ADR 0024 and #52 should be updated to say.
